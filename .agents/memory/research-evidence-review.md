---
name: Research reliability trade-offs
description: Why researched replies favor supported partial answers and independently checked corrections over plausible completion.
---

For researched factual replies, prefer an honest partial answer or uncertainty over an unsupported completion, including when the checking provider fails.

**Why:** The user approved prioritizing reliability after confident but incorrect estimates. Finding a reputable-looking link does not establish that its text supports a particular claim, date, scope or unit.

**How to apply:** Preserve the checking boundary when adding reply paths, including voice and personas. Keep ordinary conversation and roleplay free of unnecessary research and additional provider checks. Expose only sources that support the delivered answer, not every retrieved link.

A user's objection is not evidence by itself. Recheck the relevant earlier question without automatically agreeing, and ask for clarification when its target is ambiguous or inaccessible. Do not send personal historical questions or entire conversation memories to public search.

**Why:** Correcting one confident falsehood by accepting another undermines the same reliability goal, and references do not authorize access to other participants' memories.

**How to apply:** Distinguish factual corrections from language/style preferences and personal corrections; do not use a missing quote as permission to select an unrelated earlier answer.

Live rejection tests must distinguish a successfully checked rejection from an unavailable checking service.

**Why:** Both intentionally produce an uncertainty response, so checking only the final refusal can make a provider outage look like a successful factual test.

**How to apply:** Require an explicit completed-check signal in live fixtures, and include both a supported positive case and a false-correction case using fictional data.
